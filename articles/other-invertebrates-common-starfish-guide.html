Common Starfish: Habitat, Diet, Behaviour and Conservation

Scientific name: Asterias rubens

Habitat and distribution

The common starfish lives on seabeds and rocky shores of the northeastern Atlantic and nearby seas. It occurs from shallow coastal waters to deeper subtidal habitats.

Diet and feeding

It feeds on mussels, barnacles and other small invertebrates. Tube feet help it grip prey and exert force on shells.

Behaviour and adaptations

This echinoderm moves using hundreds of tiny tube feet operated by a water vascular system. It can evert its stomach through its mouth to begin digesting prey outside its body. Its five arms contain internal structures for movement and feeding. The spiny skin and water vascular system are characteristic features of echinoderms, a group that also includes sea urchins and sea cucumbers.

Life cycle

Reproduction commonly involves releasing eggs and sperm into the water. Free-swimming larvae drift as plankton before settling and transforming into juvenile starfish.

Conservation

Seabed disturbance, pollution and wider coastal ecosystem changes can affect local populations. Starfish are important predators and contribute to the balance of marine food webs.

Responsible observation

Do not remove starfish from rock pools or expose them to air. Observe them in place and follow local guidance if an animal is overturned. Never remove marine animals from their habitat or disturb them for a photograph.

Learning note: This educational profile summarises general natural history. Local populations and scientific assessments may vary; consult current research and local authorities for fieldwork.
