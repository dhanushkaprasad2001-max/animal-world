Side-striped Jackal: Habitat, Diet, Behaviour and Conservation

The side-striped jackal is a wild canid native to parts of central, eastern and southern Africa. It is generally associated with woodland, savanna, scrub and other habitats with enough cover and food.

Appearance and identification

It is a medium-sized, slender jackal with a grizzled coat and a pale stripe along each flank, usually bordered by darker fur. The tail often has a dark tip. Markings can be subtle and vary among individuals.

Habitat and distribution

The side-striped jackal is a wild canid native to parts of central, eastern and southern Africa. It is generally associated with woodland, savanna, scrub and other habitats with enough cover and food.

Diet and feeding behaviour

Its diet is varied and may include fruit, insects, small mammals, birds, reptiles and carrion. It can forage alone or in pairs, adjusting its feeding to seasonal food availability.

Social behaviour and reproduction

Side-striped jackals are often most active at night, although activity patterns vary. They use calls, scent and other signals to communicate, and typically shelter in dense cover or suitable resting sites.

Ecological role and conservation

The species is generally less conspicuous than some other African canids. Habitat change, persecution and disease can affect local populations. Accurate monitoring and protecting natural vegetation help maintain healthy ecosystems.

Observing wild canids responsibly

Observe wild animals from a respectful distance, never feed or attempt to touch them, keep pets under control, and follow local wildlife guidance. Photographs should never be worth disturbing an animal or approaching a den.

Scientific name: Lupulella adusta. Common names and taxonomic classifications can differ among authorities; consult current regional field guides when making formal identifications.
