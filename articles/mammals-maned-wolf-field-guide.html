Maned Wolf: Habitat, Diet, Behaviour and Conservation

The maned wolf is a distinctive South American canid found mainly in open grasslands, cerrado savannas and scrubby landscapes. Despite its name, it is not a true wolf and is the only living species in its genus.

Appearance and identification

It has exceptionally long legs, large ears, a reddish coat and a dark mane that can rise when the animal feels threatened. Its long limbs help it see and move above tall grass.

Habitat and distribution

The maned wolf is a distinctive South American canid found mainly in open grasslands, cerrado savannas and scrubby landscapes. Despite its name, it is not a true wolf and is the only living species in its genus.

Diet and feeding behaviour

The maned wolf is an omnivore with an unusual amount of fruit in its diet. The lobeira fruit is especially important in some regions, alongside rodents, insects, birds and other small animals.

Social behaviour and reproduction

Maned wolves are generally solitary outside the breeding period and communicate through scent marking and vocal calls. Pairs may share a home range while spending much of their time apart.

Ecological role and conservation

Conversion of native grasslands, road mortality, fires, disease and conflict with people threaten the species. Maintaining natural savanna, wildlife crossings and responsible domestic-dog vaccination can support its survival.

Observing wild canids responsibly

Observe wild animals from a respectful distance, never feed or attempt to touch them, keep pets under control, and follow local wildlife guidance. Photographs should never be worth disturbing an animal or approaching a den.

Scientific name: Chrysocyon brachyurus. Common names and taxonomic classifications can differ among authorities; consult current regional field guides when making formal identifications.
