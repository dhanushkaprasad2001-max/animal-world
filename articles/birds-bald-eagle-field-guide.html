Birds Explained: The Bald Eagle's Adaptations and Life

Birds are warm-blooded vertebrates with feathers, beaks and wings. All living bird species lay eggs, and their feathers help with flight, insulation, camouflage, display and waterproofing. Not every bird flies: ostriches, emus, kiwis and penguins are examples of flightless birds.

Meet the bald eagle

The bald eagle (Haliaeetus leucocephalus) is a large bird of prey native to North America. Adults are recognised by their white head and tail, dark brown body and yellow bill. Juveniles are mostly brown and take several years to develop adult plumage.

Hunting and diet

Bald eagles are opportunistic carnivores. Fish are an important part of their diet, but they also take waterbirds and small mammals and may scavenge carrion. Their strong feet and curved talons help them grip prey, while a hooked bill tears food into manageable pieces.

Flight and senses

Broad wings allow eagles to soar on rising air currents and travel long distances while conserving energy. Their eyesight is highly developed, helping them detect potential prey from far away. Feather structure supports controlled flight and helps keep the body insulated.

Nests and young

Pairs build large nests, often in tall trees near water, and may reuse or add to them over multiple breeding seasons. Both parents contribute to incubation and care of the young. Nest success depends on food availability, weather and disturbance around the nesting site.

Recovery and remaining pressures

Bald eagle populations suffered severe declines in the twentieth century, including from exposure to the pesticide DDT, habitat loss and persecution. Legal protection, restrictions on DDT and conservation work supported a major recovery. Human disturbance, lead ammunition fragments and environmental contaminants can still affect some populations.

Bird-friendly places

People can help birds by protecting wetlands and mature trees, reducing pesticide use, keeping cats indoors or supervised, making windows more visible to birds, and providing native plants. Never approach an active nest or attempt to handle a wild bird.

Identification tip: The adult bald eagle's white head is distinctive, but young bald eagles do not show the full white head-and-tail pattern.
