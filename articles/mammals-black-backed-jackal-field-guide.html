Black-backed Jackal: Habitat, Diet, Behaviour and Conservation

The black-backed jackal is a small, alert canid found in eastern and southern Africa. It occupies a range of open habitats, including savannas, grasslands, semi-deserts and coastal scrub.

Appearance and identification

Its most recognisable feature is a dark saddle of black and silver hairs running along the back, contrasting with reddish sides and legs. It has large ears, a pointed muzzle and a bushy tail.

Habitat and distribution

The black-backed jackal is a small, alert canid found in eastern and southern Africa. It occupies a range of open habitats, including savannas, grasslands, semi-deserts and coastal scrub.

Diet and feeding behaviour

Black-backed jackals are opportunistic omnivores. They feed on insects, rodents, reptiles, birds, fruit and carrion, and may scavenge remains left by larger predators.

Social behaviour and reproduction

Pairs often defend territories and cooperate in raising pups. Vocal calls, scent marking and pair behaviour help maintain contact and defend resources.

Ecological role and conservation

The species is adaptable but can come into conflict with livestock owners and may be affected by persecution, disease and habitat change. Protecting poultry at night and securing waste can reduce conflict without harming wildlife.

Observing wild canids responsibly

Observe wild animals from a respectful distance, never feed or attempt to touch them, keep pets under control, and follow local wildlife guidance. Photographs should never be worth disturbing an animal or approaching a den.

Scientific name: Lupulella mesomelas. Common names and taxonomic classifications can differ among authorities; consult current regional field guides when making formal identifications.
