Fish Explained: Clownfish and Their Reef Homes

Fish are aquatic vertebrates that generally breathe through gills and use fins to move. The group includes jawless fish, cartilaginous fish such as sharks and rays, and the much larger group of bony fish. Fish live in freshwater, seawater and, in some cases, move between the two.

Clownfish and anemones

Clownfish are small reef fish in the subfamily Amphiprioninae. The familiar orange-and-white clownfish is the ocellaris clownfish (Amphiprion ocellaris). It lives among the tentacles of certain sea anemones, which provide shelter from many predators.

A remarkable partnership

The relationship between clownfish and host anemones is a form of mutualism: both partners can benefit. The fish gains protection, while its movements and waste may provide food or other benefits to the anemone. The exact relationship varies by species and conditions.

How fish breathe

Water passes over gill surfaces where dissolved oxygen moves into the blood and carbon dioxide moves out. Many fish have a swim bladder that helps control buoyancy, although sharks and some other groups use different strategies.

Reproduction and social structure

Many clownfish live in groups associated with a host anemone. In several species, the largest individual is the breeding female and the next largest is the breeding male. If the female dies, the breeding male can change sex and take her place. This is called sequential hermaphroditism.

Reef ecosystems under pressure

Coral reefs are affected by warming seas, marine heatwaves, ocean acidification, pollution, destructive fishing and physical damage. Protecting reefs requires reducing greenhouse gas emissions, improving water quality and supporting sustainable fisheries and tourism.

Responsible aquarium choices

Never release aquarium fish or plants into the wild. If keeping marine fish, research their full care requirements, choose responsible captive-bred sources where available, and ensure the aquarium is properly established and maintained. A photograph of a clownfish should never encourage collecting wild animals irresponsibly.

Remember: Fish do not all breathe or reproduce in exactly the same way. Their diversity is one of the most remarkable features of aquatic life.
