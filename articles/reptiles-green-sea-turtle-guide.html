Reptiles Explained: The Green Sea Turtle's Ocean Journey

Reptiles are ectothermic vertebrates with skin covered in scales or scutes. Most lay eggs, although some species give birth to live young. Snakes, lizards, turtles, tortoises and crocodilians are all reptiles, and their adaptations allow them to live in deserts, forests, rivers and oceans.

The green sea turtle

The green sea turtle (Chelonia mydas) is a large marine reptile found in tropical and subtropical waters around the world. It has a streamlined shell and paddle-like flippers that make long-distance swimming efficient. Despite spending most of its life at sea, females must return to land to lay eggs.

Diet changes with age

Young green turtles are more omnivorous, taking a variety of small marine animals and plant material. Many adults feed mainly on seagrasses and algae. Healthy seagrass meadows are therefore important feeding habitats, and turtles can help shape these ecosystems through grazing.

Breathing and diving

Sea turtles breathe air with lungs and must surface to breathe. They can remain underwater for extended periods while resting or foraging, but dive duration varies with activity and conditions. They cannot breathe underwater like fish.

Nesting and hatchlings

Females crawl onto sandy beaches, dig nests and lay eggs. Incubation temperature influences hatchling development and sex ratios in many sea turtle species. After hatching, young turtles make their way to the sea, where they face natural predators and environmental challenges.

Conservation challenges

Green sea turtles face threats that include accidental capture in fishing gear, loss of nesting beaches, coastal lighting, marine debris, vessel strikes and degradation of seagrass habitats. Conservation measures include turtle-friendly fishing practices, protecting nesting beaches, reducing plastic pollution and monitoring populations.

How to help

Keep beaches clean, avoid disturbing nesting turtles or hatchlings, use wildlife-safe lighting near nesting areas and follow local conservation guidance. Never buy products made from protected turtle shells or disturb animals for photographs.

Important: A sea turtle is a reptile, not an amphibian. It breathes air and has scaly skin, even though it is adapted to life in the ocean.
