Essential Daily Care for Dogs and Cats

Good companion-animal care is built from consistent everyday routines. Dogs and cats have different behavioural and nutritional needs, but both depend on people for safe shelter, suitable food, clean water, preventive healthcare and a life that allows natural behaviours.

Provide suitable nutrition

Choose a complete and balanced food formulated for the animal's species and life stage. Feeding amounts depend on age, body condition, activity and health. Follow product guidance as a starting point, measure portions and ask a veterinarian to help adjust the diet. Avoid sudden diet changes unless medically necessary.

Dogs are omnivorous and can use a properly formulated balanced diet. Cats are obligate carnivores and require specific nutrients found in animal tissues or carefully formulated cat foods. Never assume a dog food is suitable for a cat.

Keep fresh water available

Provide clean water every day and wash bowls regularly. Cats may prefer multiple water stations away from litter trays and food. Changes in drinking can signal illness and should be discussed with a veterinarian.

Make time for exercise and enrichment

Dogs need walks, sniffing opportunities, play and reward-based training appropriate to their age and physical condition. Cats need interactive play that allows stalking and pouncing, scratching surfaces, hiding spaces and safe elevated resting places. Rotate toys to keep activities interesting.

Maintain hygiene and grooming

Brush according to coat type, trim nails safely, and check ears, skin, paws and teeth. Dogs may need bathing depending on coat and lifestyle; cats usually groom themselves but long-haired cats often need regular combing. Use products intended for the correct species and ask a veterinarian before using medicated treatments.

Prevent illness and parasites

Work with a veterinarian on vaccinations, parasite prevention, dental care, microchipping and routine health checks. Never use a dog flea treatment on a cat unless the label specifically states it is safe for cats; some dog products contain ingredients that are highly toxic to cats.

Create a safe home

  • Store medicines, cleaning products, pesticides and toxic foods securely.
  • Keep electrical cords, small swallowable objects and sharp items out of reach.
  • Provide species-appropriate resting areas and protect animals from extreme heat and cold.
  • Use secure carriers for cats and appropriate restraints for dogs during travel.
  • Introduce children and other pets gradually and supervise their interactions.

Notice changes early

Changes in appetite, thirst, breathing, movement, toileting, grooming or behaviour can be signs of a health problem. Seek prompt veterinary advice, and treat breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, severe bleeding or suspected poisoning as emergencies.

Important: This is general educational information. Your veterinarian can tailor preventive care, nutrition and treatment to your animal's age, health and local disease risks.

Build a routine that lasts

Responsible ownership means planning for ordinary daily needs as well as unexpected illness, travel, ageing and emergency costs. A predictable, kind routine helps companion animals feel secure and gives owners a better chance to notice changes before they become serious.
