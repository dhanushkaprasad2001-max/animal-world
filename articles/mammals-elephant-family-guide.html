Mammals Explained: The African Elephant and Its Family Life

Mammals are warm-blooded vertebrates that have hair or fur at some stage of life and feed their young with milk produced by mammary glands. Most give birth to live young, although a small group of mammals lay eggs. Mammals occupy almost every major habitat, from tropical forests and grasslands to deserts, mountains and oceans.

The African elephant as a mammal

The African savanna elephant (Loxodonta africana) is the largest living land animal. Its trunk is a remarkable combination of nose and upper lip, used for breathing, smelling, drinking, gathering food, touching and communication. Large ears help release heat, while broad feet support the animal's great weight.

Family groups and communication

Female elephants and their young commonly live in family groups led by an experienced female known as a matriarch. Family members maintain close social bonds through touch, calls, scent and low-frequency rumbles. Adult males often spend more time alone or in loose groups, although their social relationships remain important.

What do elephants eat?

Elephants are herbivores. They feed on grasses, leaves, bark, roots and fruit, changing their diet with the season and local vegetation. They need large quantities of food and water, so access to connected habitats is essential. By moving seeds and changing vegetation, elephants can influence the ecosystems in which they live.

Growth and reproduction

Elephant pregnancy lasts about 22 months, one of the longest gestation periods among mammals. A female usually gives birth to one calf. Calves depend on their mothers and other family members for protection and learning, and may remain with the family for many years.

Threats and conservation

Habitat conversion and fragmentation, conflict with people, and illegal killing for ivory continue to threaten elephant populations. Conservation requires connected landscapes, effective protection, safe wildlife corridors and practical ways for communities to reduce conflict. African savanna elephants are classified as Endangered by the IUCN; conservation status should always be checked against the latest assessment.

How to observe elephants responsibly

When viewing elephants in the wild, keep a safe distance, never block their route, do not feed or touch them, and follow qualified local guides. Responsible wildlife tourism places animal welfare and habitat protection ahead of close encounters.

Key fact: An elephant's trunk contains tens of thousands of muscle units and can perform both powerful and delicate movements.

Explore more mammals

Other mammal groups include primates, bats, rodents, carnivores, hoofed mammals, whales, dolphins and marsupials. Each group has distinct adaptations and ecological roles.
