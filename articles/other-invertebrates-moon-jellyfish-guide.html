Moon Jellyfish: Habitat, Diet, Behaviour and Conservation

Scientific name: Aurelia aurita species complex

Habitat and distribution

Moon jellyfish occur in coastal seas and bays across many regions, often in sheltered waters. They can tolerate a range of conditions, and seasonal blooms may occur when food and environmental conditions are favourable.

Diet and feeding

They capture plankton, fish eggs and tiny aquatic organisms using mucus and stinging cells on their oral arms and tentacles.

Behaviour and adaptations

The translucent bell pulses to move through the water. Stinging cells called cnidocytes help capture prey. The familiar four horseshoe-shaped structures visible through the bell are reproductive organs. Their simple body plan is adapted to drifting and pulsing in the water column. They have no bones, brain or centralised heart, but possess a nerve net that coordinates movement and responses.

Life cycle

The life cycle includes a free-swimming medusa and an attached polyp stage. Polyps can produce young medusae, allowing populations to persist through changing seasons.

Conservation

Coastal pollution, warming, habitat change and shifts in marine food webs can influence jellyfish abundance. Jellyfish are also part of marine food webs and provide food for some predators.

Responsible observation

Do not touch jellyfish, including stranded individuals. Follow local beach warnings and seek medical advice for significant stings. Never remove marine animals from their habitat or disturb them for a photograph.
