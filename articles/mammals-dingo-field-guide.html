Dingo: Habitat, Diet, Behaviour and Conservation

The dingo is a wild canid found across much of mainland Australia, with a complex history linked to people and the continent's ecosystems. It lives in deserts, grasslands, forests and other habitats.

Appearance and identification

Dingoes are lean, athletic canids with erect ears, a tapered muzzle and a bushy tail. Coat colour varies and may be ginger, sandy, black-and-tan, cream or other patterns. Not every free-roaming dog in Australia is a pure dingo.

Habitat and distribution

The dingo is a wild canid found across much of mainland Australia, with a complex history linked to people and the continent's ecosystems. It lives in deserts, grasslands, forests and other habitats.

Diet and feeding behaviour

Dingoes are carnivores and opportunistic scavengers. Their prey ranges from insects and small mammals to larger animals such as kangaroos, depending on local conditions. They may also consume carrion.

Social behaviour and reproduction

Dingoes can live alone, in pairs or in family groups. Social structure and pack size depend on food availability, breeding and human disturbance. They communicate through scent, body posture and vocalisations.

Ecological role and conservation

Dingoes have ecological importance as top predators in many Australian landscapes, but they also create challenges for livestock producers. Conservation and management need to recognise regional differences, genetic complexity and the needs of local communities.

Observing wild canids responsibly

Observe wild animals from a respectful distance, never feed or attempt to touch them, keep pets under control, and follow local wildlife guidance. Photographs should never be worth disturbing an animal or approaching a den.

Scientific name: Canis lupus dingo. Common names and taxonomic classifications can differ among authorities; consult current regional field guides when making formal identifications.
