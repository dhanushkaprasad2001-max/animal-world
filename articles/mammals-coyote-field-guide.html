Coyote: Habitat, Diet, Behaviour and Conservation

The coyote is a highly adaptable wild canid native to North America. It occupies deserts, grasslands, forests, mountains and many urban-edge environments. Its success comes partly from flexible behaviour and a broad diet.

Appearance and identification

Coyotes are usually smaller and more lightly built than grey wolves, with a narrow muzzle, upright ears and a bushy tail often carried low. Coat colours range from grey and brown to reddish or pale tones.

Habitat and distribution

The coyote is a highly adaptable wild canid native to North America. It occupies deserts, grasslands, forests, mountains and many urban-edge environments. Its success comes partly from flexible behaviour and a broad diet.

Diet and feeding behaviour

Coyotes eat rodents, rabbits, insects, fruit, carrion and, where available, larger prey. Their diet changes with local conditions and season. They may hunt alone or cooperate with a mate or family group.

Social behaviour and reproduction

Breeding pairs can form long-term bonds and raise pups in dens. Young coyotes learn hunting and social behaviour from adults, and family groups may remain together for a period before dispersing.

Ecological role and conservation

Coyotes play ecological roles as predators and scavengers. In towns and suburbs, secure rubbish, removing outdoor pet food and supervising small pets can reduce unwanted encounters. Never feed or approach a wild coyote.

Observing wild canids responsibly

Observe wild animals from a respectful distance, never feed or attempt to touch them, keep pets under control, and follow local wildlife guidance. Photographs should never be worth disturbing an animal or approaching a den.

Scientific name: Canis latrans. Common names and taxonomic classifications can differ among authorities; consult current regional field guides when making formal identifications.
